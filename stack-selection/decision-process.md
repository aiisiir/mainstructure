# Stack Selection Decision Process

1. **Define workload:** users, traffic shape, data volume/sensitivity, latency, consistency, availability, integrations, offline needs, and growth assumptions.
2. **Define constraints:** team, skills, deployment, budget, compliance, delivery dates, vendor restrictions, SEO, localization, accessibility.
3. **Generate a short list:** normally 2–4 viable candidates, including the status quo or buy/reuse option when reasonable.
4. **Apply hard constraints:** remove non-viable candidates and record why.
5. **Score remaining candidates:** agree weights first, score with evidence, state confidence, and run a spike for high-impact unknowns.
6. **Check operational reality:** who patches, deploys, monitors, restores, upgrades, and supports it?
7. **Check exit strategy:** export data, migrate, replace dependencies, and expected switching costs.
8. **Decide and record:** create an ADR, including alternatives, trade-offs, risks, assumptions, and review triggers.
9. **Revisit on triggers:** major workload change, team change, end-of-life, security incident, cost variance, or evidence that a core assumption was wrong.

The framework recommends; the accountable human owner approves. Never claim one stack is universally best.
